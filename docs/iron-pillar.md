# Iron Pillar of Delhi — Ancient Metallurgy

Source: Wikipedia article "Iron pillar of Delhi" (CC BY-SA 4.0, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_pillar_of_Delhi, retrieved 2026). Condensed extract for demo retrieval corpus.

The iron pillar of Delhi is 7.21 metres high and weighs more than six tonnes. Built by Chandragupta II (reigned c. 375-415 CE), it now stands in the Qutb complex at Mehrauli in Delhi. It was possibly erected outside the Udayagiri Caves and moved to Delhi by Anangpal Tomar in the 11th century, or later by Iltutmish in the 13th century.

Its bell capital is 306 mm. The pillar resists corrosion due to a passive film of crystalline iron hydrogen phosphate forming on high-phosphorus iron. Ancient smiths used no lime and wood with high phosphorus content, giving over 0.1 percent phosphorus (average 0.25 percent) versus under 0.05 percent in modern iron. In 1,600 years the film grew one-twentieth of a millimetre.

The oldest inscription names king Chandra, identified as Chandragupta II, devotee of Vishnu, recording a Vishnu standard on Vishnupada hill. A second short inscription is linked to Tomara king Anangpal. An indentation at 4 metres is attributed to a cannonball, possibly ordered by Nadir Shah in 1739.
