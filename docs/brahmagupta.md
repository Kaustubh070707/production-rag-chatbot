# Brahmagupta — Ancient Indian Mathematician

Source: Wikipedia article "Brahmagupta" (CC BY-SA 4.0, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmagupta, retrieved 2026). Condensed extract for demo retrieval corpus.

Brahmagupta (c. 598-668 CE) was an Indian mathematician and astronomer, credited as the first person to formalize the concept of zero. He authored the Brahmasphutasiddhanta (628 CE) and the Khandakhadyaka (665 CE), and gave the first clear description of the quadratic formula.

Born in Bhillamala (modern Bhinmal, Rajasthan), he lived and worked there, then moved to Ujjain, a major astronomy centre. He was the head of the Ujjain observatory and died in 668 CE.

The Brahmasphutasiddhanta has 1008 verses in 25 chapters on algebra, arithmetic, geometry and number theory. It gives rules for zero and negative numbers: a debt minus zero is a debt, zero minus zero is zero. It gives the quadratic formula solution and methods for cyclic quadrilaterals, including Brahmagupta's formula for area and Brahmagupta's theorem on diagonals.

He gave the sum of squares n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 and sum of cubes as the square of the sum. He solved Diophantine equations with the pulverizer method and gave recurrence relations for Pell's equation. His sine table used radius 3270, and he devised a second-order interpolation formula for sines in 665 CE.

Brahmagupta described gravity as attraction, using the term gurutvakarshanam: all heavy things fall to earth by nature. His texts reached Baghdad under Caliph Al-Mansur, translated as Sindhind, spreading the decimal number system westward through Al-Khwarizmi.
